★ Novel feature: Funder Drift Intelligence Engine (FDIE)
Detects, scores, and explains how funding entities change their priorities, requirements, geographies, and openness to partnership over time — making the intelligence system dynamic rather than static.
The core problem FDIE solves
Funders are not static actors. A company that funded women's skilling in Rajasthan in FY 2021 may now fund climate adaptation in Uttarakhand. An NGO that got rejected two years ago on CSR-1 grounds may now be eligible. A funder that only funded direct implementation is quietly opening to intermediary grants. None of this is visible in any existing database. FDIE makes these movements legible and actionable.

FDIE technical design
Drift dimensionSignal sourceDetection method
Sector driftBRSR Schedule VII allocations, Annual report CSR chapter, program descriptionsYear-wise sector allocation vector → cosine distance between baseline window and current window
Geography driftCSR portal state/district tags, program descriptions, implementing agency locationsGeo coverage set diff over time; weighted by spend proportion
Requirement driftToR documents, eligibility criteria, rejection/acceptance pattern dataNLP diff on requirement text fields; registration list set diff; threshold/band changes
Openness driftImplementing mode field, NGO partner roster, accepting-proposals flag, ToR languageTime-series of implementing-mode + proposal-acceptance changes; semantic tone analysis of ToR
Drift score formula (simplified)
SECTOR_DRIFT = 1 − cosine_similarity(sector_vector_t, sector_vector_t−n)
GEO_DRIFT = |current_geo_set Δ baseline_geo_set| / |union|
REQ_DRIFT = (added_reqs × 1.2 + dropped_reqs × 0.8 + modified_reqs × 0.5) / total_reqs
OPEN_DRIFT = logit_change(P(accepts_proposals)) + mode_change_flag

COMPOSITE_DRIFT = 0.35·SECTOR + 0.25·GEO + 0.25·REQ + 0.15·OPEN
Weights are configurable per user profile (e.g. an NGO seeking new funders may weight OPEN_DRIFT higher).
FDIE alerts — what gets triggered
Openness window alert
Funder shifts from closed to open — notify matching NGOs before cycle opens
Sector realignment alert
Funder drops a sector from priority → NGOs in that sector can anticipate reduced funding
Requirement relaxation alert
Funder drops a registration requirement → previously ineligible NGOs may now qualify
Geography expansion alert
Funder adds new state/district → local NGOs in that region get priority notification
Why this is hard to copy
FDIE requires longitudinal, field-level data collection from the moment of database inception — you cannot reconstruct drift history retroactively unless you archive snapshots. Any competitor entering later starts with a 2–3 year handicap. The proprietary moat compounds over time: the longer the system runs, the richer the drift signal, the better the alerts, the more defensible the intelligence.

Key risks & validation questions
Data quality risk: CSR portal data is often incomplete or lagged. Must verify against BRSR and annual reports independently.
Validation Q1
Will NGOs pay for this? What is the willingness-to-pay ceiling for a grant-intelligence subscription?
Validation Q2
Can drift detection be validated against known funder behavior changes (e.g. post-COVID sector shifts)?
Validation Q3
Is the matching model accurate enough to trust? Need ground-truth data from successful grant applications.
Validation Q4
What is the update cadence required for the data to stay actionably fresh — monthly? quarterly?
